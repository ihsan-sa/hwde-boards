\hstitleblock{PCB-0018 Rev A \textperiodcentered\ bldc-motor-driver \textperiodcentered\ September 2026}%
{Owner's guide: 3-phase BLDC motor driver}%
{What the board does, why its gate drive, current sensing, power loop and
copper are built the way they are, how to wire it up, what is on it, and what
still has to happen before anyone orders it.}

PCB-0018-A is a 4-layer, 78 x 78 mm driver for a three-phase brushless
(BLDC or PMSM) motor. It runs from a 10--28 V bus (12--24 V nominal) and is
meant for 10 A RMS per phase continuous and 20 A peak, without a heatsink. Six
60 V N-channel FETs form three half-bridges, a DRV8300D gate driver switches
them, three 3 mOhm shunts with INA240 amplifiers measure the phase currents, and
an STM32G431 runs the control loop (sensored or sensorless field-oriented
control). It is a 3-phase BLDC driver designed from a general spec. The only
reference was a published demo board seen in one frame of a short video, and
nobody published that board's spec or parts, so the spec here is ours.

All five build gates pass: ERC, placement, routed DRC, the design checks and
DFM. The design checks pass with 390 waivers, explained below. Nothing has been
built or measured, and nothing has been ordered. The 10 A RMS rating is a design
target, not a proven one.

Power comes in on TP101 (VM) and TP102 (GND) and passes a reverse-polarity FET
(Q101, controlled by U101, an LM74500) before it reaches the bus, the SMCJ28A
TVS and two 470 uF electrolytics. An LM5164 buck makes +5 V from the bus; a
TPS61040 boost makes the +12 V gate supply from +5 V, and a TLV75733 LDO makes
+3.3 V for the MCU and the current-sense amplifiers. The MCU drives the gate
driver with six PWM lines. Phase and bus voltages come back to the MCU through
150 k / 10 k dividers (1:16), so the 45 V clamp reads 2.8 V at the ADC.

The board ships with no firmware. First power-up should be from a
current-limited bench supply at 12 V with no motor connected: check +5 V,
+12 V and +3.3 V, then program the MCU over SWD. The firmware must hold all six
driver inputs low until +12 V is up, cap the PWM duty near 95\% so the bootstrap
capacitors recharge, and pulse the low sides before the first high-side turn-on.
Set the comparator trip on the current-sense outputs before a motor is ever
connected, since that is the only overcurrent protection. Then connect the motor
and raise the current limit in steps while watching the FET and capacitor
temperatures.

the board was not recorded either.
